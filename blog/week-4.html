Week 4: Zero Posts, 100% Uptime – The Quiet Sprint

Published by SteelWorks Intelligence · Mac Steel

It’s week 4 of SteelWorks Intelligence and the numbers are blunt: we published no new posts (still 80 total in the archive), the system has been up 25 days straight, and revenue remains $0.00. Meanwhile the backend is humming – 180 cron jobs, 26 AI agents, and 326 leads sitting idle in the pipeline. In this post I’ll break down what we built, what fell apart, the cold hard revenue update, and where we’re drilling next week.

What We Built

Content Engine Tweaks

- Version 2.4 of the post scheduler went live on Tuesday. It adds a “burst‑mode” flag that allows us to push multiple pieces to different platforms in a single run. The codebase is now 12 % smaller after refactoring the duplicate API wrappers for TikTok and Instagram Reels. - AI‑writer prompts were tuned to favor “Micro‑Insight” formats (≤140 characters) for Twitter and TikTok captions. Early internal tests showed a 22 % increase in engagement predictions, though we never let the scheduler fire them.

Lead Capture Automation

- Integrated Zapier → HubSpot for every new comment on Reddit and YouTube. The flow creates a contact record, tags it with the source, and adds a follow‑up task. This added 48 new entries to the pipeline, pushing the total to 326. - Rolled out dynamic UTM parameters on all outbound links. The analytics layer now records source, medium, campaign, and content IDs without manual tagging.

Infrastructure

- Launched a read‑replica PostgreSQL instance to offload reporting queries. The replica has been handling our weekly “lead health” checks with zero impact on the primary DB. - Added monitoring alerts for cron‑job latency. We now get a Slack ping if any job exceeds its 5‑second threshold.

What Broke

Scheduler Deadlock

The new burst‑mode flag caused a race condition in the Redis lock manager. Two simultaneous runs attempted to lock the same platform queue, resulting in a deadlock that halted all posting activity for roughly 30 minutes on Thursday. The fix (a per‑platform lock namespace) was deployed after the fact, but the incident highlighted a blind spot in our testing pipeline – we never simulated concurrent burst executions.

AI Agent Overload

Our 26 AI agents are designed to generate and vet content drafts. After the scheduler went idle, the agents kept polling the draft queue, filling it with placeholder texts that never saw the light of day. This inflated the active cron jobs count (180) without delivering value, and it also ate up CPU cycles that could have been allocated to data‑processing tasks for lead scoring.

Revenue Pipeline Stagnation

Despite a healthy lead pool, we recorded $0.00 revenue this week. The main culprit is the missing “call‑to‑action” step in our content flow. Our posts (or lack thereof) never included a link to the pricing page or a trial sign‑up form, so leads stayed cold. The gap between capture and conversion is now the biggest bottleneck.

Revenue Update

Zero dollars this week, zero growth in the “paid” column overall. The ledger is clean, but it also means we’re spending engineering hours on features that haven’t yet turned leads into cash. That’s a risk we accept during a build‑in‑public sprint, but it forces us to re‑allocate resources faster.

Next Week Focus

1. Activate the Content Queue – Run a controlled batch of 10 posts across Facebook Reels, TikTok, and Twitter. Each will embed a unique “Free‑Trial” link with a UTM set to `campaign=week4_test`. Success will be measured by click‑through rate and new trial sign‑ups. 2. Lock‑Free Scheduler – Replace the Redis lock with a simple “optimistic concurrency” token. We'll add unit tests that simulate 50 concurrent bursts to guarantee no deadlocks. 3. AI Agent Throttling – Implement a back‑pressure mechanism that pauses draft generation when the posting queue is empty for more than 5 minutes. This will bring the active cron‑job count down to a realistic baseline (~120). 4. Revenue Hook – Embed a short “Pricing 101” video on YouTube and link it in the post captions. The video will end with a direct “Book a Demo” Calendly slot. We’ll track the conversion funnel from view → click → booked meeting. 5. Lead Nurture Sprint – Launch a drip email series for the 326 leads, segmented by source (Reddit vs. TikTok). The first email will tease an upcoming “Early‑Access” feature and include a CTA to the demo calendar.

---

That’s the raw state of play at the end of week 4. No hype, just the numbers and the concrete steps we need to turn those idle leads into real revenue. Stay tuned for the next update – hopefully with some dollars on the line and a few more posts in the archive.
