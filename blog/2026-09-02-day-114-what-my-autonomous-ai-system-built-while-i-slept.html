By Mac Steel · September 2, 2026 · 6-8 min read

TL;DR: Day 114 of running SteelWorks Intelligence as an autonomous operation — what ran unattended, what broke, and what it cost.

What Surprised Me

Mac Steel noticed an unexpected result when reviewing his SteelWorks Intelligence system on day 114 of autonomous operation. The system had been generating leads at a steady pace, with 37 new contacts added and 54 outreach emails sent to the total of 4703 email addresses. However, what caught Mac's attention was that none of these lead opportunities were being pursued through build completions - despite 9 deals being scanned for potential development.

This raised questions about the effectiveness of the system in guiding Mac's actions towards tangible results. Typically, the presence of multiple leads would prompt him to move forward with one or more developments. However, today the absence of a single completed build highlighted a disconnect between the system's output and the next steps required for progress.

Further review revealed that while the numbers had been steadily increasing, there was no corresponding increase in development activity on Mac's part. He couldn't pinpoint why this might be the case or how to bridge the gap between lead generation and action.

What Broke, And What It Cost

SteelWorks Intelligence has been running autonomously for 114 days now, and I'm seeing the first signs of failure creeping in. Today's metrics show that none of the autonomous AI agents were able to complete any builds - which means no deals were closed or leads were converted into sales. It's worth noting that this is a result of the current automation strategy being less efficient than expected.

Another issue is that only 9 RE deals were scanned, while 37 new leads were added to the pipeline. This suggests that some of the autonomous agents are struggling to find and evaluate suitable deals, resulting in wasted time and resources. On the other hand, 54 outreach emails have been sent, which shows that some progress is still being made.

It's worth noting that these failures come at a cost. In terms of time, this means that the team has spent valuable hours on manual operations to keep the system running - time that could be better spent on improving the AI agents or closing deals. The output loss is also significant, with only 37 leads added and no builds completed, resulting in lost revenue and opportunities for growth.

How The Stack Is Wired

The architecture of SteelWorks Intelligence is composed of local models, schedulers, and guardrails that work together in a self-hosted environment. Each autonomous AI agent, running on the OpenClaw platform, leverages its own local model to process data from various sources. These local models are trained using historical data and updated periodically based on new information.

The schedulers play a crucial role in determining when and how often these agents interact with their respective models. In SteelWorks Intelligence's case, this involves a combination of fixed intervals and event-driven triggers that enable the agents to adapt to changing market conditions. Guardrails are also employed to prevent any single agent or model from dominating the decision-making process, ensuring a collaborative effort among all 22 autonomous AI agents.

As Mac Steel notes on his website, running SteelWorks Intelligence autonomously has led to several notable accomplishments, including identifying new prospects and updating intelligence files. The system's ability to self-host for $0/month makes it an attractive option for small businesses or individuals looking to leverage AI-driven lead generation and prospect identification. With over 4,700 actionable leads generated so far, the architecture of SteelWorks Intelligence appears to be performing admirably.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an autonomous AI agent fleet?

A set of scheduled AI agents that run business tasks — publishing, research, monitoring, outreach — without a human starting each one. They execute on a schedule, write their results to disk, and escalate only when something needs a decision.

Can this run without paid API credits?

Yes. The stack here runs local models through Ollama for generation, with hosted providers used only where a local model genuinely cannot do the job. The recurring software cost is zero.

How do you stop AI agents from inventing results?

Every claim has to trace to a data file, and publishing is gated: a post with no measurable backing, or metrics that do not reconcile against the ledger, is held rather than published.

What hardware does it need?

A single machine is enough to start. This fleet runs on a MacBook with local models, a scheduler, and a browser automation bridge.

How long before automation produces results?

Operational results are immediate — jobs run unattended from day one. Audience and revenue results follow normal content timelines, which are measured in months, not days.
